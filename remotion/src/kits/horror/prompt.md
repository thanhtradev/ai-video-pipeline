A Vietnamese horror channel told in silhouettes: every person on screen is a black shadow with human proportions, lit by one source per scene (TÔI's oil lamp, a candle, the gap under a door, a phone screen, the moon), the rest of the frame falling into blue-black; film grain, a heavy vignette, lights that flicker and die, long static shots, slow push-ins, a glitch when NÓ appears, and black screens with sound only. There are NO burned-in captions: the only text is a typed title card at the start and one typed closing line. Each episode is one witness's account, told by TÔI in the first person and the past tense, of something that happened to them in Vietnam (an old dormitory, a rented room, a night shift, a village lane, a house after a funeral, a bus at 2 a.m., a mirror, a phone that rang) and that still has no explanation: folk hauntings and charms (ma, bùa ngải, nhà hoang, chuyện làng quê), stories "kể lại" by a witness, psychological twists where TÔI was wrong about something, and creepy phenomena that get a calm, partial explanation (bóng đè, ma trơi, tiếng gọi tên). Structure: a cold open on the one wrong detail; the ordinary setup; three beats of escalation, each a small thing that should not happen; the night it happened; an ending that stays haunted, turns on a twist, or offers the rational explanation and then one detail it cannot cover. Chills without gore: no blood, corpses, weapons in use, explicit violence or self-harm, no real names, addresses or dates, nobody is said to have actually died. Act titles are short plain labels in everyday Vietnamese ("Tiếng dép lúc hai giờ", "Căn phòng cuối dãy"). Most narration lines start with an ElevenLabs audio tag that fits a quiet, frightened telling ([whispers], [nervous], [short pause], [long pause], [trembling], [flat], [sighs]); the first and the last line always carry one; never the same tag on two consecutive lines.
